Mechanism audit of study27 farming experiments
============================================

Scope and interpretation
------------------------
This audit uses source contracts, completed statistics and retained replay
stdout. Outcome selection is reported separately. These observations do not
establish that an individual camp was stolen, that a death happened while
farming, or that any particular target rejection caused a win or loss.

Broad live-camp guards
---------------------
The six broad guards run immediately before the final live-camp engage. They
leave earlier champion-fight decisions, the pre-turn spell sweep, rallying,
unknown-camp approach, rewards and recall unchanged. Their forecast uses ready
spell metadata, a basic-attack reach heuristic, raw monster strength and a
conservative envelope of legal camp-closing landings. It is not exact path or
post-armour fight simulation. An optional dangerous landing can block an engage
even if another landing would have worked, and any already damaged camp can be
abandoned by these original broad variants.

On the small official-map smoke panel, periodic logs recorded at least
238/541/799 blocks for outnumbered/volley/contested hold responses, versus
115/168/209 for their back-off counterparts. One contested-hold collision game
recorded 146 blocks versus 18 with back-off against the same spar and side.
Repeated waiting is therefore a plausible failure mode worth distinguishing
from useful repositioning. Both example games were wins; the counters do not
prove lost camps or causality. The complete development results, rather than
these smoke counts, determine retention.

The checks and blocks are cumulative per champion body and sampled periodically.
Only bodies that have already blocked are logged. Blocks are lower bounds;
blocks/checks is not a global trigger rate. Do not sum periodic cumulative
samples as independent actions.

Resource costs
--------------
The broad policy adds SDK inspections and a bounded landing evaluation. It has
an entry fuel reserve, a cached self-health value, and a loop fuel exit. These
costs can still influence other existing fuel-dependent paths; equivalent
tactical intent does not imply identical runtime behaviour. On the matched
54-game official smoke panel, every policy and the original had the same 30
periodic low-fuel samples, and no bot faults or repeated initializations were
reported. This does not mean there were no caught SDK errors or no unlogged
low-fuel turns.

The geometry re-entry module initially scanned support during recent danger
even when no hold was active. Before those policies played any games, a separate
immutable activity amendment added s.hold >= 0. Only reentry16_active and
reentry32_active were evaluated. The earlier registered inactive variants were
not selected from match outcomes.

Corners components and roster dependence
---------------------------------------
The verified team v39 source contains three independently tested behaviours:
a held-camp lone-entry restriction, relaxation of the normal nonheld-camp
danger exclusion when a buddy is near, and 45% HP fleeing on Corners. The
nonheld-camp relaxation is not the same operation as restricting lone entry.

Original held geometry exists only when the caster-roster flag is enabled and
an eligible central altar has at least two gem groups within the hold radius.
In this source the caster flag is detected from a roster containing Stellar.
Thus a held-camp rule is conditional on roster and geometry; its presence in
source does not prove it runs in every match. The v39 buddy count includes
living allies within six tiles without the new generic module's health,
channel or spawn-healing exclusions. Its Corners flee threshold also applies
to noncaster bodies, whose original flee threshold can differ from casters.

Generic held-camp re-entry
-------------------------
The active generic variants use existing held geometry on every map, not a
Corners fingerprint. They reject a travel target near recent shared danger
without an available nearby buddy sufficiently close to that camp. Fresh
rewards are exempt. Existing committed-camp execution, nonheld-camp danger
exclusion, fight, flee, recall and shared-memory layout remain unchanged.
Chebyshev proximity still cannot prove that a buddy can cross walls or help in
time; remembered pack danger can also outlive the relevant contact.

Existing diagnostic coverage
----------------------------
The completed re-entry stage contains 4,048 games. Its 16 retained replays are
all collision: eight for each active policy. Every sampled candidate log prints
"[hold] altar 27,20 groups 2", confirming active held geometry outside Corners.
There are no explicit re-entry rejection or danger-age counters in these frozen
logs. Consequently no observed Corners-versus-other-map rejection frequency can
be calculated from the retained evidence.

Periodic target and position trajectories differ from original study27 in the
matched collision spar replays. Those trajectories demonstrate changed play,
but they do not identify which choices the new clause rejected. Counts of
target=-1 also include healing, banking and other priorities; they must not be
labelled idle farming or conceded camps. Replay duration and intervening turns
further limit snapshot comparisons.

Detailed retained-log checks and SHA-verified replay coverage are recorded in
reentry-log-audit.json. No new games were executed for this mechanism audit.
